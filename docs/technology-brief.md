# Technology Brief

## 1. Selected Technology
**Technology:** Multimodal Artificial Intelligence (AI)
**Model:** Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training (CLIP)
**Supporting Techniques:** Natural Language Processing (NLP) and Computer Vision

## 2. Concepts
### 2.1 Natural Language Processing (NLP)
Natural Language Processing (NLP) is a field of artificial intelligence that enables computers to process, analyse and interpret human language.
Traditional text-based misinformation detection approaches may rely on surface-level linguistic features without considering the relationship between textual information and accompanying images.
Although modern NLP models can capture semantic meaning, text-only approaches cannot directly determine whether a news image accurately represents its accompanying caption.

### 2.2 Computer Vision
Computer Vision is a field of artificial intelligence that enables computers to process and extract meaningful information from visual data.
In this project, computer vision supports the extraction of visual features from uploaded news images.

### 2.3 Multimodal Artificial Intelligence
Multimodal AI refers to artificial intelligence systems that process and analyse multiple types of data, such as images and text.
Unlike text-only approaches, multimodal AI enables the analysis of relationships between visual and textual information.
This capability is particularly relevant to detecting potential inconsistencies between news images and their accompanying captions.

### 2.4 Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training (CLIP)
CLIP is a vision-language model developed to learn relationships between images and natural language descriptions.
The model uses two separate encoders:
1. **Image Encoder:** Extracts visual features from an image.
2. **Text Encoder:** Converts textual information into numerical representations.
Both representations are mapped into a shared embedding space, allowing their semantic similarity to be measured.
A similarity score indicates how closely the visual and textual representations align.
However, semantic similarity alone cannot establish whether a news image has been used in the correct historical or geographical context.

## 3. System Workflow
1. The user uploads a news image.
2. The user enters the accompanying caption or headline.
3. The image and text are processed using CLIP.
4. CLIP generates image and text embeddings.
5. The system calculates semantic similarity between the embeddings.
6. A defined decision rule is applied to assess potential misinformation risk.
7. The system displays the consistency assessment and risk classification.

## 4. Frameworks, Models, Methods and Tools
### 4.1 CLIP
CLIP is the primary vision-language model proposed for image-text semantic consistency analysis.
It generates visual and textual representations that can be compared to identify potential semantic inconsistencies.

### 4.2 Hugging Face Transformers
Hugging Face Transformers provides access to pretrained models and processing tools.
It can support the integration of a pretrained CLIP model into the proposed system.

### 4.3 PyTorch
PyTorch is a machine learning framework that supports model execution and tensor-based computations.
It can be used to process model inputs and perform inference using CLIP.

### 4.4 Cosine Similarity
Cosine similarity is a mathematical method used to measure the similarity between two numerical vectors.
In this project, it can be used to compare image and text embeddings generated by CLIP.

## 5. Suitability of the Technology
CLIP-based multimodal analysis is suitable for the proposed project because it considers both visual and textual information.
The approach supports the identification of potential semantic inconsistencies that may not be detected through text-only analysis.

### Benefits
1. **Cross-Modal Analysis:** Enables comparison between images and their accompanying captions.
2. **Automated Assessment:** Supports preliminary identification of potentially misleading image-text relationships.
3. **Pretrained Model Availability:** Allows the use of existing vision-language models without developing a model entirely from scratch.
4. **Accessibility:** A web-based interface can allow users to submit image-text pairs and view consistency assessments.

## 6. Limitations
### 6.1 Contextual Limitations
CLIP may generate high semantic similarity scores for images and captions that describe similar concepts, even when the image originates from a different event, location or time.
Therefore, a high similarity score does not necessarily indicate that a news claim is factually accurate.

### 6.2 Dataset Limitations
The effectiveness of the proposed approach depends on the suitability of available image-text datasets.
Differences between training datasets and Malaysian news-related content may affect the reliability of the analysis.

### 6.3 Classification Limitations
A semantic similarity score is not equivalent to a verified probability of misinformation.
The decision rule used for potential misinformation-risk classification must be evaluated using suitable labelled examples.

## 7. Comparison with an Alternative Approach
### Text-Only NLP Classification
Traditional text-only NLP classification approaches analyse linguistic features within textual content.
Although these approaches can identify patterns associated with misinformation, they cannot directly compare a caption with its accompanying image.

### CLIP-Based Multimodal Analysis
CLIP-based analysis processes both images and text to assess their semantic relationship.
This makes it more suitable for the proposed project, which focuses on identifying potential inconsistencies between news images and captions.
However, additional evidence would still be required to independently verify the factual accuracy and original context of news content.
