## 1. Problem Statement
Existing text-based misinformation detection approaches may rely on surface-level linguistic features or analyse textual content without considering its relationship with accompanying images. Although modern Natural Language Processing (NLP) techniques can capture semantic information, text-only analysis cannot fully assess whether an image accurately represents the context of a news claim. This limitation creates challenges in detecting out-of-context misinformation, where genuine images are paired with misleading captions or headlines. Therefore, cross-modal semantic alignment using vision-language models is explored to identify potential inconsistencies between visual and textual information.

## 2. Supporting Evidence
**Bernama (2025)** reported a viral false-news case concerning Pamela Ling's death investigation, highlighting the circulation of misinformation in Malaysia. However, this case illustrates the broader misinformation problem rather than directly demonstrating an image-text mismatch.

**Hu et al. (2024)** discussed the importance of image-text matching in multimodal fake news detection, as text-only analysis may overlook inconsistencies between visual and textual information.

**Bondielli et al. (2024)** highlighted the importance of labelled multimodal datasets for developing and evaluating misinformation detection systems.

## 4. Project Scope
### In Scope
- Uploading news images and entering captions or headlines.
- Cross-modal semantic consistency analysis using CLIP and NLP techniques.
- Potential misinformation-risk classification.
- Presentation of image-text consistency results.
- Usability and functionality testing.

### Out of Scope
- Real-time social media monitoring.
- Video and audio misinformation detection.
- Image manipulation detection.
- Independent verification of the factual accuracy of news claims.
